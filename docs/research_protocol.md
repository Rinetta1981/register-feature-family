# Research Protocol

## Project title

Register Is a Feature Family, Not a Dial

## Protocol status

**Draft version 0.1**

This protocol is being written before dataset generation, model training,
or inspection of model activations.

It will be reviewed and frozen before the confirmatory experiments begin.
Any later changes will be recorded as dated amendments.

## 1. Primary research question

Does a transformer language model represent linguistic register as one
general formal–informal direction, or as a family of causally separable
features?

The initial study examines four dimensions of register:

1. lexical formality;
2. syntactic directness;
3. politeness mitigation;
4. epistemic stance.

Speaker–addressee power relation will be included as a contextual social
variable.

## 2. Scope of the first study

The first study will use a small decoder-only transformer trained on a
controlled synthetic language.

Because the dataset will be generated by us, the linguistic variables
that produced every example will be known.

A later study will investigate whether the central findings transfer to
an open pretrained language model.

## 3. Controlled variables

### Operational definition of register

For this synthetic study, register is operationalized as the joint
configuration of lexical formality, syntactic directness, politeness
mitigation, and epistemic stance in a specified speaker–addressee power
relation.

Register will therefore not be treated as a single observable label.
Each component will be generated, measured, and evaluated separately.

### 3.1 Propositional content

Propositional content is the action or claim communicated by an
utterance.

Example:

- send the report;
- review the document;
- schedule the meeting.

Content will be held constant when register dimensions are compared.

### 3.2 Lexical formality

Lexical formality concerns the conventional formality of vocabulary.

Example contrast:

- less formal: "check the report";
- more formal: "review the report".

Lexical pairs will be selected to preserve the underlying action as
closely as possible.

### 3.3 Syntactic directness

Directness concerns how explicitly the utterance presents its requested
action or claim.

Example contrast:

- direct: "Send the report."
- indirect: "Could you send the report?"

Directness will be varied while preserving the requested action.

### 3.4 Politeness mitigation

Politeness mitigation concerns linguistic devices that reduce the force
of a request or disagreement.

Example contrast:

- unmitigated: "Send the report."
- mitigated: "Please send the report."

Mitigation will be distinguished from syntactic indirectness whenever
the controlled grammar permits this distinction.

### 3.5 Epistemic stance

Epistemic stance concerns the speaker's expressed degree of certainty.

Example contrast:

- categorical: "The result demonstrates a difference."
- hedged: "The result may indicate a difference."

The proposition will remain constant while the expression of certainty
changes.

### 3.6 Speaker–addressee power relation

Power relation describes the relative institutional position of the
speaker and addressee.

The initial levels will be:

- lower-power speaker addressing higher-power addressee;
- equal-power participants;
- higher-power speaker addressing lower-power addressee.

Power relation is a contextual variable. It must not automatically be
treated as equivalent to politeness or formality.

## 4. Hypotheses

### H1: Multidimensional representation

A representation containing separate dimensions will predict lexical
formality, directness, mitigation, and epistemic stance on unseen
combinations better than a single shared formal–informal direction.

### H2: Correlation produces entanglement

Models trained on strongly correlated register dimensions will develop
less separable representations than models trained on balanced,
independent dimensions.

### H3: Causal selectivity

Intervening on a dimension-specific representation will change the
corresponding register dimension more strongly than it changes
non-target dimensions or propositional content.

## 5. Training-data regimes

### 5.1 Balanced regime

The four register dimensions will vary independently and occur with
approximately equal frequency.

Power relations will also be balanced.

Purpose:

The balanced regime tests whether the model can learn distinct register
dimensions when the training data makes them identifiable.

### 5.2 Correlated regime

Conventional bundles such as formal, indirect, mitigated, and hedged
language will occur frequently.

Counterexamples will still occur often enough for the dimensions to be
distinguishable.

The initial target distribution will be approximately:

- 80 percent conventional bundles;
- 20 percent counterexamples.

Purpose:

The correlated regime tests whether realistic statistical associations
encourage the model to combine several dimensions.

### 5.3 Confounded regime

Several register dimensions will always occur together during training.

For example, formal vocabulary may always occur with indirect and
mitigated syntax.

Purpose:

The confounded regime is a negative-control condition. Because the
training data does not distinguish the variables, we should not expect
the model to recover them as independent dimensions.

## 6. Evaluation splits

### 6.1 IID split

The IID split will contain unseen examples drawn from combinations that
also occurred during training.

Purpose:

This tests whether the model learned the ordinary training
distribution.

### 6.2 Compositional OOD split

The compositional out-of-distribution split will contain familiar
register features in combinations that were withheld from training.

Each individual feature level will still have appeared during training.

Purpose:

This tests whether the model learned reusable dimensions rather than
memorizing complete register bundles.

### 6.3 Lexical OOD split

The lexical out-of-distribution split will contain content words or
content frames that were withheld from training.

Purpose:

This tests whether the model can apply a register distinction to new
semantic content.

## 7. Behavioral measures

The primary behavioral measures will be:

1. content accuracy;
2. lexical-formality accuracy;
3. directness accuracy;
4. mitigation accuracy;
5. epistemic-stance accuracy;
6. complete-combination accuracy.

The synthetic grammar will make these properties automatically
measurable.

Results will be reported separately for IID, compositional OOD, and
lexical OOD examples.

## 8. Behavioral gate

Mechanistic analysis will only be conducted on models that demonstrate
a sufficiently stable behavior to explain.

For at least two of three training seeds, the balanced-condition model
must achieve:

- at least 95 percent content accuracy on IID examples;
- at least 90 percent content accuracy on OOD examples;
- at least 90 percent accuracy for each register dimension on IID
  examples;
- at least 75 percent accuracy for each register dimension on
  compositional OOD examples.

If the behavioral gate is not passed, the failed result will be
reported. We will not claim to have identified a mechanism for a
behavior the model has not learned reliably.

## 9. Representational comparison

Two main explanations will be compared.

### 9.1 Single-dial explanation

A rank-one representation will use one shared internal direction to
predict all four register dimensions.

### 9.2 Feature-family explanation

A multidimensional representation will allow different internal
directions to predict the four register dimensions.

The comparison will be evaluated on held-out examples, especially the
compositional OOD split.

A more complex model will not automatically be considered better.
Performance, dimensionality, generalization, and causal evidence will
all be considered.

## 10. Causal evaluation

Candidate representations will be tested using interventions such as:

- activation patching;
- feature suppression;
- feature amplification;
- interchange interventions.

For every intervention, we will measure:

- change in the intended register dimension;
- change in the other register dimensions;
- preservation of propositional content;
- comparison with random and no-operation controls.

Finding that a probe can decode a variable will not be treated as proof
that the model uses that variable.

## 11. Random seeds

The main training conditions will initially use three predetermined
random seeds:

- 0;
- 1;
- 2.

Results will not be reported from only the most favorable seed.

## 12. Confirmatory analyses

The following analyses are planned as confirmatory:

1. comparison of rank-one and multidimensional representations;
2. comparison of balanced, correlated, and confounded training regimes;
3. evaluation on IID, compositional OOD, and lexical OOD splits;
4. causal-selectivity testing for all dimensions that pass the
   behavioral gate;
5. replication across the three predetermined seeds.

## 13. Exploratory analyses

The following will initially be exploratory:

- the exact sparse-autoencoder architecture;
- the number of sparse features;
- model-width and model-depth sweeps;
- identification of individual attention heads and MLPs;
- selection of one circuit for detailed investigation;
- replication in a pretrained language model.

Exploratory findings may generate new hypotheses, but they will be
clearly distinguished from the confirmatory tests.

## 14. Stopping and amendment rules

1. Mechanistic analysis will stop if the behavioral gate is not passed.
2. Failed or null results will be retained and reported.
3. Thresholds will not be changed after results are inspected merely to
   obtain a preferred conclusion.
4. Changes made after the protocol is frozen will be documented in a
   dated amendment.
5. Pilot runs used to debug the software will not be included as
   confirmatory evidence.
6. Confirmatory data will remain separate from data used for feature
   discovery or code development.

## 15. Reproducibility requirements

Every reported experiment will record:

- the data-generator version;
- dataset checksums;
- model configuration;
- random seed;
- dependency versions;
- training configuration;
- checkpoint identifier;
- analysis configuration.

The repository will contain automated tests and a documented
reproduction procedure.

## 16. Current limitations

This first study will use a simplified synthetic language.

Its findings will not by themselves establish how register is
represented in large pretrained language models or in natural human
interaction.

The synthetic study is intended to provide controlled ground truth and
a validated interpretability method before a real-model replication.
